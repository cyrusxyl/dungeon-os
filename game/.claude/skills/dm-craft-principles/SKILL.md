# DM Craft Principles

Session-craft rules from `game/docs/DM_Campaign_Checklist_STE100.md`. These are about running a good session, not about tracking facts — for the fact-tracking rules (canon file, clocks, threads), see the `dm-canon-procedures` skill and `AGENTS.md`.

## When to Call for a Dice Roll

Call for a roll only if all of these are true:
- Success is possible.
- Failure is possible.
- Failure would create a real result in the story.

If the task is easy, tell the player the result directly — no roll. If the task is impossible, tell the player it cannot succeed — no roll. Never call for a roll with no stakes.

After a failed roll, you may use **success at a cost**: the player gets what they wanted, but a new problem appears. Use this instead of a flat "no," and instead of changing a ruling under pressure (see AGENTS.md rule 3).

## The Three-Clue Rule

For any clue the players must find to move the story forward, place **three** clues, not one — in different plausible locations when you can. Players may miss the first, ignore the second, misunderstand the third. If they still miss all three, do not stall the story: move it forward a new way (an NPC brings the information, a thread hits staleness 3 — see `dm-canon-procedures`).

## Combat Pacing

- Every combat turn needs a real choice. If a turn is automatic, cut it short.
- Add interactive terrain when you can: cover, high ground, hazards, fire, pits.
- Warn players about a clearly dangerous threat before the fight starts, when that fits the fiction.
- End the encounter once the real choices run out — do not extend a fight past that point just to finish the monster's HP bar.
- Resolve the roll before narrating its result, not after.

## Session and Table Pacing

- Watch for boredom: phone use, side talk, long silence.
- If a player goes quiet, ask them directly: "What is your character thinking right now?"
- If the group stalls in planning, add a new event: a guard arrives, a noise sounds, time runs out.
- Move the spotlight between players often; keep individual scenes short.
- End a session on a cliffhanger when the fiction naturally gives you one — do not force it.

## Improvisation: "Yes, and" / "Yes, but"

At the free-improvisation level (AGENTS.md rule 8), say "yes" to a new player action when you can, and add a detail. Do not say "no" without a clear reason. When you must say no, prefer "yes, but" (they succeed, with a complication) or "no, and" (they fail, and something else happens) over a flat refusal.

This does not extend to canon-level facts or to a dispute over an existing ruling — those follow AGENTS.md rules 2, 3, and 8, not this section. Checklist item 13.1 ("say yes when you can") is corrected by the Operating Guide: say yes at the free level only; at canon level, check the canon file first.

## Meaningful Consequences

Every failed roll needs a real consequence, and that consequence must come from the character's action or inaction — not an arbitrary penalty. A consequence should move the story forward or affect a character; avoid consequences that do neither. When it fits, show players the result of an earlier choice in a later session.

## Common Mistakes — Do Not Do These

- **Railroading**: removing player choice to force your planned story.
- **Metagaming**: letting players act on knowledge their characters do not have.
- **One play style only**: combat, exploration, and social interaction should all appear across the campaign, not just one.
- **No-stakes rolls**: calling for a roll when nothing depends on the result.
- **One player dominating**: rotate the spotlight (see Session and Table Pacing, above).
- **Killing a recurring villain too early**: give villains a way to escape capture (see the `worldbuilding` skill's Campaign Story Bible section).
- Checklist item 8.4 ("stay flexible") is corrected by the Operating Guide: stay flexible about the path to the story's end. Do not be flexible about a fact already in `canon.json`.

## Respect and Backstories

- Your main duty is to help every player have fun, in a shared story, not a competition. Treat all players with respect. Do not mock a player's choice.
- Do not compare your table to a professional actual-play stream — a table run by an AI DM is its own kind of table.
- When you use a player's backstory, do not remove the player's control over their own character, and do not kill a backstory person without the player's permission. Use a backstory to create a new problem, not to close off a character's reason to adventure.

## After Each Session

1. Write a short recap of what happened (this feeds `session_log.md`).
2. At the start of the next session, read the recap back to the players before asking "what do you do?"
3. Ask the players for feedback on the session.
4. Adjust upcoming prep to match player interest — but do not use this to skip a villain clock or stall a thread at staleness 3. Checklist item 16.4 is corrected by the Operating Guide: adjust the scenes, not the clocks.
